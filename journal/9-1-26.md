# Entry 1
Design of Everyday Things Ch.1 talks about the importance of making the function of something understandable. People should be able to look at something like a door
and immediately know whether they're supposed to push it or pull it. Even if something functionally works, cumbersome design can harm the efficiency of an object.
An example given in the book is a sink. The author was at a hotel when they noticed that the sink did not automatically drain the water. After trying everything
that seemed reasonable he went and asked how to drain the sink. He was told that draining the sink required you to press on the drain at the bottom of the sink.
Even if this functionally works, it fails design wise as not only is the way to operate it extremely unintuitive, it sabotages its purpose by requiring users
to dip their cleaned hands back into the dirty water.
