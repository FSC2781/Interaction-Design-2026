# Entry #3
Chapter 2 of _The Design of Everyday Things_ discusses forced constraints. Forced constraints is when a design halts operation if one of the steps needed fails. The example the book brings up is
for cars. If you are unable to start the car with the key, the car will not allow you to operate any other part of the car until you successfully start the car. In this case, forced constraints
act as a form of security to allow your car not to be take by someone who does not have the key.
